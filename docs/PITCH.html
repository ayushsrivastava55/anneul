Describe the job.
Anneal builds the agent.

Teams ship agents on vibes. You tweak a prompt, it looks better on the four cases you checked, and nothing tells you whether it actually improved. Anneal gives that process a scoreboard, and refuses changes that do not earn their place.

You give itA job in one sentence, the tools it may use, and a handful of examples. Five questions in a browser.
It gives you backAn agent, the evidence it works, and a written record of every change it refused.

Syndicate by Maximor, Track 1. Ayush Srivastava and Tanush Singhal.

01

The loop

The same six steps whatever the job is, and you watch each one happen.

DesignWires your goal and tools into several different agents. One alone, a planner and a doer, a doer with a reviewer.
Try itRuns every design over your examples and scores each one, task by task.
Find the faultsGroups the failures by what actually went wrong, and names the repair each one calls for.
Fix oneApplies that repair. It never spends the same repair on the same fault twice.
Prove it helpedRe-runs both versions on tasks held back from every earlier step. If the change does not win there, it is gone.
Make it cheaperWalks the winner down to cheaper models for as long as the score holds.

On one round the challenger scored 0.800 against the incumbent's 0.767, and Anneal threw it away, because it made three serious mistakes where the old one made one. A system that only reports its wins is not measuring anything.

Four agents through the identical loop with no code written for any of them, plus two more built from scratch through the five questions to check it works on a job it had never seen. Accuracy moved both ways; every case where it fell is listed in the repo.
